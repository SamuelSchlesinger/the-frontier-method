# Combinatorial simplification and restriction accounting

[Comparison overview](../index.md). Baseline: arbitrary fan-in-two Boolean DAGs,
the full binary basis `B2`, unrestricted sharing, and `s` positive-arity gates.
Constants are free; a remaining output negation costs one gate. Formula leaf size,
De Morgan gate size, branching-program size, and query depth are different resources.
Write `L = 4.562497678925021...` for the existing unrestricted coefficient.

The most plausible contribution from this family is a **restriction-plus-terminal
coverage theorem**, using the frontier theorem as a substantial terminal bound.
Formula shrinkage and subfunction counts supply useful diagnostics, but their
strongest numerical bounds do not directly measure `B2` gates. The proofs below
make three failed transfers precise and give an accounting target for any positive
transfer. These are paper deductions, not additional Lean theorems or priority claims.

## Models, mechanisms, and possible contact points

| Method and exact resource | Mechanism | Frontier contact and missing bridge |
|---|---|---|
| Gate elimination; exact `B2` or a specified subbasis, gate count | Substitute inputs; amortize deleted gates and auxiliary measures | Improve the circuit before compilation; substitutions must preserve target hardness and pay for newly built signals [gkst16][gkst16] |
| Switching; bounded-depth AND/OR/NOT circuits, depth and size | A restricted narrow CNF/DNF has a shallow decision tree with high probability | Certify simple residual terminals; no bounded-depth hypothesis follows from linear `B2` size [hastad86][hastad86] |
| Threshold anti-concentration; restricted depth, gate or wire complexity | Random restrictions make weighted threshold gates constant or highly biased | Candidate distribution-aware terminals; exact forcing, approximate bias, arbitrary weights, and majority gates are different hypotheses [kanewilliams16][kanewilliams16] [chensrinivasan18][chensrinivasan18] |
| Shrinkage; De Morgan formulas, literal occurrences | A restriction deletes repeated occurrences and simplifies tree structure | Use only after a paid conversion to formula terminals; both parity gates and DAG sharing obstruct a direct transfer [subbotovskaya61][subbotovskaya61] [hastad98][hastad98] |
| Andreev and structured projections; De Morgan formula size | Preserve a hard residual function while a competing formula shrinks | Search for target-preserving projections and measurable DAG savings; preservation alone gives no shrinkage theorem [andreev87][andreev87] [filmus23][filmus23] |
| Nechiporuk; full-basis formula leaves or branching-program nodes | Count distinct cofactors on disjoint input blocks and sum local occurrence costs | Frontier states encode **sets** of cofactors; one-way interfaces and bounded reuse are extra hypotheses [nechiporuk66][nechiporuk66] [beame16][beame16] |
| Khrapchenko/weighted adversaries; De Morgan formula leaves | Opposite-output pairs force repeated variable occurrences | Candidate terminal demand, not a gate charge; one XOR gate can combine many already-shared sensitive directions [khrapchenko71][khrapchenko71] [laplante05][laplante05] |
| Sensitivity/certificates; query measures | Local changes or monochromatic subcubes constrain observations | Rule out overly simple terminals; high sensitivity and certificate complexity coexist with `n-1`-gate parity circuits [huang19][huang19] |
| Composition/direct sums; model-specific resources | Preserve independent demands through a composition | Need non-reuse accounting and preservation of the all-partition hard-set hypothesis; ordinary block composition can destroy that hypothesis |

## Gate elimination: retain its accounting, enlarge its terminals

After constants and unary operations are propagated, an essential binary gate is
either parity-type or AND-type, up to input/output complements. Of the sixteen
truth tables, two are constant, four depend on exactly one input, two are
parity-type, and eight are AND-type. Fixing an incident input removes its binary
gate, with a possible final unary gate; only the AND-type class has a controlling
input value that makes the output constant. Immediate fanout alone therefore does
not capture cascades through a parity front end.

The literature permits weighted measures, multi-variable substitutions, and
complementary branches. Its substantive obligation is hardness for the sources
generated by those substitutions [gkst16][gkst16]. A bound proved over `U2`, which
excludes XOR and XNOR, is not a bound over `B2`; a numerical coefficient cannot be
imported while changing this basis. Local gate-elimination limitations also concern
specified measures and substitution schemes, not every hybrid with a stronger
terminal theorem [golovnev16limits][golovnev16limits].

The [existing restriction note](../../communication/restrictions.md) already has
the high-fanout and safe nonlinear-successor rules, fractional branch accounting,
the exact deficit `5d-s`, and a low-fanout obstruction. Its affine-prefix and
syndrome continuations are established starting points here, not new proposals.
The remaining issue is coverage of the residual circuits, including an arbitrary
nonlinear continuation of a dense affine interface.

**A permissible expansion of the rule set.** Literal identifications
`x_i = x_j` or `x_i = 1-x_j` require no new binary gates. More general literal
projections are injective affine maps whenever every new variable occurs. The
existing affine-pullback theorem therefore preserves sumset bias under them.
Dimension drops by the rank loss, not by the number of syntactic occurrences.
An affine substitution involving many other variables also preserves the target,
but computing that parity must be charged unless an existing signal supplies it.
A nonlinear substitution has no such justification from sumset bias alone.

The [nonlinear comparison](nonlinear.md) separately checks weighted gate elimination
and circuit depth reduction, including their variety-source hypotheses and the
difference between charging `s` and `s-n`.

## A cumulative restriction-and-terminal lemma

**Proved accounting lemma.** Consider an exact circuit for a sumset-extractor
target on `n` inputs, with `log2 K=o(n)` and bias `b<=b_0<1/2` for fixed `b_0`. Apply
injective affine restrictions, constructing a correct circuit after each step.
Step `i` loses `q_i` ambient dimensions and removes a **net** `g_i` gates, including
all construction and normalization costs. Stop at dimension `d >= kappa*n` for
fixed `kappa>0`. Suppose a valid terminal argument proves

```
s_terminal >= L*d + H_terminal - r(n),    r(n)=o(n),
```

uniformly for the terminal family; `H_terminal` is an additional, proved terminal
bonus and may be zero. Then

```
s_initial >= L*n + sum_i (g_i - L*q_i) + H_terminal - r(n).       (1)
```

Proof: `d=n-sum q_i` and `s_terminal=s_initial-sum g_i`; substitute and rearrange.
Affine pullback retains sumset bias, hence rectangle-freeness, and a linear
residual dimension retains density and `log K=o(d)`. Thus the old frontier lower
bound supplies the terminal with `H_terminal=0`. If re-encoding changes the circuit
model, its conversion cost must enter `g_i` or the terminal inequality explicitly.
Unused but unfixed coordinates remain in `d` throughout.

This elementary telescoping identity is useful because it asks for **cumulative
surplus above `L`**, not a five-gate deletion at every step. It also avoids proving
that every terminal independently achieves coefficient five. For coordinate
restrictions deleting either four or five gates, let `t=rho*n` be the number of
steps and let a fraction `a` delete five. Their certified surplus is

```
rho * (4 + a - L) * n.
```

It is positive precisely when `a > L-4 = 0.5624976789...`. For example,
`rho=1/2, a=3/5` would add `0.0187511605...n` to the old bound, **if a coverage
theorem supplied such a branch on every candidate circuit**. This is a benchmark
for a missing theorem, not an improved unrestricted coefficient. The same
calculation allows occasional losses below four if later deletions or a terminal
bonus pay them back.

**Exact missing theorem.** Find fixed `delta,kappa>0` and a sound restriction/
terminal scheme such that every circuit computing the target, in the size range
under consideration, admits a branch ending at `d>=kappa*n` with
`sum(g_i-L*q_i)+H_terminal >= delta*n`. Equation (1) then gives
`s >= (L+delta)n-o(n)`. A distribution over eligible branches with this expected
surplus also suffices for the exact lower bound, by averaging.

For an **average-case** lower bound, the existence of one good branch is
insufficient. One must bound the weighted sum over all leaves. The existing
restriction note gives exactly that moment composition law; a terminal bonus in
an exact lower bound is not automatically a bound on its transition moment.
This is where exact and average-case coverage must remain separate.

## Random restrictions, switching, and shrinkage

For a width-`k` CNF or DNF under independent restrictions retaining each variable
with probability `p`, switching gives a tail of the form `(O(pk))^t` for residual
decision-tree depth at least `t`. Iteration removes a bounded number of AND/OR
layers [hastad86][hastad86]. For unrestricted `B2`, depth can be linear, and a
frontier layout does not turn gates on a cut into a narrow CNF or DNF for free.
Unrolling its transition relation can already cost exponentially many states.
The plausible use is local: prove a residual region has bounded depth/width,
pay its interface, and insert its simplification into (1).

Subbotovskaya obtained shrinkage exponent `3/2`; Andreev combined shrinkage with
a hard residual table to obtain a superquadratic explicit formula lower bound
[subbotovskaya61][subbotovskaya61] [andreev87][andreev87]. Håstad proved, for De
Morgan formula leaf size `ell`,
`E[ell_restricted] = O(p^2 log(1/p)^(3/2) ell + p sqrt(ell))` for `0<p<=1/2`, yielding
`n^(3-o(1))` lower bounds [hastad98][hastad98]. Filmus–Meir–Tal extend shrinkage
to structured random projections and obtain cubic, up to logarithmic factors,
De Morgan formula lower bounds for an explicit function in `AC0` [filmus23][filmus23].
The common mechanism separates preservation of the target from simplification
of the competing representation. Injective literal projections solve the first
obligation for our target; the second remains a new DAG theorem.

Threshold-circuit restrictions supply a distinct mechanism. Kane--Williams use
Littlewood--Offord anti-concentration for gate and wire lower bounds at depth two
and a specified majority-topped depth-three model. Chen--Santhanam--Srinivasan
prove average-case lower bounds for arbitrary-weight threshold circuits of each
fixed depth with slightly superlinear wire budgets, using adaptive restrictions
and biased-gate analysis [kanewilliams16][kanewilliams16]
[chensrinivasan18][chensrinivasan18]. A highly biased gate can be replaced only
with an error budget; it is not an exactly forced gate. For our method this suggests
a distribution-aware terminal certificate, after a paid conversion from the
residual `B2` circuit and proof of the required independence/read-multiplicity
conditions. Shared selectors and XOR front ends are controls against silently
assuming those conditions. The terminal must enter the existing cumulative or
weighted branch accounting; a threshold gate is not a unit-cost `B2` operation.

**Sharp falsification of a direct shrinkage transfer.** Let `PARITY_n` have its
optimal `n-1`-gate `B2` circuit. After a `p`-random restriction, the number `M` of
live variables is binomial. A nonconstant parity on `M>=2` essential inputs
requires and has exactly `M-1` binary gates; this follows from the fan-in-two
input-count bound and an XOR tree. Therefore, even ignoring a possible output NOT,

```
E[s_restricted] >= E[max(M-1,0)] = np - 1 + (1-p)^n.             (2)
```

For `n=m^3, p=1/m`, this is `m^2-O(1)`, while the claimed formula-style upper
bound would be `O(m log(m)^(3/2)+sqrt(m))`. The transfer is false even for an XOR
**formula**, before sharing is considered. Separately, De Morgan DAGs compute
parity using at most `4(n-1)` positive-arity gates by reusing the two inputs to
each four-gate XOR gadget, whereas De Morgan formulas require `n^2` leaves.
Changing the basis at constant cost therefore does not repair the sharing issue.

## Nechiporuk: cofactor demand is not frontier bit demand

For a block `B`, let `R_B(f)` count distinct functions of `B` obtained by fixing
the other inputs. Nechiporuk sums blockwise occurrence costs: full-basis formulas
give a lower bound proportional to `sum_B log R_B`, while general deterministic
branching programs have a weaker logarithmic denominator. The corresponding
classical method ceilings are respectively `Theta(n^2/log n)` and
`Theta(n^2/log^2 n)` [nechiporuk66][nechiporuk66] [beame16][beame16]. Substituting
such a polynomial program-size lower bound into an exponential frontier compiler
would give only logarithmic demand on its exponent, losing the current linear
demand from rectangle-freeness.

**Proved sharp state/cofactor lemma.** Suppose, at a fixed partition, the accepting
relation has a sound and complete decomposition
`f^(-1)(1) = union_{z in Z} A_z × B_z`, with `N=|Z|`. Then the number `R` of
distinct cofactors on the first side is at most `2^N`. For a fixed second-side
input `y`, its accepting cofactor is exactly `union_{z:y in B_z} A_z`; there are
at most `2^N` choices. Thus `w` frontier bits give only

```
R <= 2^(2^w),    or    w >= log2 log2 R    when R>1.             (3)
```

This cannot be improved using only the stated representation. For
`INDEX(a,z)=z_a`, take `q` address bits `a` and `2^q` data bits `z`. The `2^q`
rectangles `{a} × {z:z_a=1}` are disjoint. Varying `z` realizes all
`2^(2^q)` truth tables on `a`, so equality holds. This example is a transfer
counterexample; it is not proposed as a rectangle-free hard target.

There is a useful stronger special case: if `f(x_B,y)=H(x_B,m(y))` for a
deterministic `b`-bit message `m(y)`, then `R_B<=2^b`. The message must be fixed
without knowing `x_B`, and together they must determine the output. This is the
one-way interpretation underlying Nechiporuk's method [klauck07][klauck07]. A
frontier is generally an interactive consistency interface, not such a message.
Even when a one-way interface is certified, summing its cost over blocks requires
an explicit bound on how often the same gate or signal is charged.

## Sensitivity, boundary pairs, and adversaries

Khrapchenko's bound takes `A subset f^(-1)(0)`, `B subset f^(-1)(1)`, and the
Hamming-distance-one pairs `E subset A×B`, and gives De Morgan formula leaf size
at least `|E|^2/(|A||B|)` [khrapchenko71][khrapchenko71]. For parity this is
exactly `n^2`, despite its `n-1`-gate `B2` circuit. Laplante–Lee–Szegedy generalize
such arguments using `sumPI` and `maxPI`, whose squares lower-bound De Morgan
formula size [laplante05][laplante05]. Their compositional behavior belongs to
that occurrence-sensitive resource, not arbitrary DAG gate count.

Sensitivity, block sensitivity, and certificate complexity can reject proposed
query-simple terminals. Huang's sensitivity/degree theorem connects these local
function measures, without an additive gate charge [huang19][huang19]. A target
nonconstant on every coordinate subcube of dimension at least `k` has every
certificate of size at least `n-k+1`: a smaller certificate would leave such a
monochromatic cube. This deduction can contradict a shallow decision-tree
terminal. It cannot establish a large gate coefficient: parity already has
sensitivity, block sensitivity, and certificate complexity all equal to `n`.

An adversary-based terminal remains worthwhile only with a proved conversion
from its circuit to the exact formula/query resource being lower-bounded, paying
for duplicated gates. A global sum of sensitive-edge demands across cuts has no
such conversion. Repeatedly observing a shared parity does not require repeatedly
recomputing it. Neither the unweighted nor weighted pair count supplies the
missing reuse control by itself.

## Composition and direct sums must preserve both obligations

Formula composition can amplify occurrence demand; the projection work gives
KRW-type results for specified inner functions with tight adversary bounds
[filmus23][filmus23]. No general circuit direct-sum theorem is invoked here.
In a DAG, disjoint input blocks do not themselves partition the intermediate
gates. A valid additive charge must give disjoint witnesses or fractional charges
whose total load on every gate is at most one. Shared-input multi-output examples
make the issue immediate: computing `(h(x),h(x))` costs no more than computing
`h(x)` once, so outputwise lower bounds cannot simply be added.

**Proved obstruction to the obvious hard-function composition.** Let
`F(x,y)=H(g(x),h(y))`. If `H(a,b)=1`, then
`g^(-1)(a) × h^(-1)(b)` is a monochromatic accepting rectangle at the block split.
Whenever both fibers have size at least `K`, `F` is not `K`-rectangle-free.
Balanced hard inner functions on large disjoint blocks therefore destroy the
frontier demand hypothesis under ordinary nonempty Boolean outer composition.
This says nothing against composition lower bounds in other models; it rules out
using them here without a new demand theorem or a target that mixes the blocks.

## Research decisions and falsification gates

| Priority | Concrete target | First falsification test and stopping condition |
|---|---|---|
| Highest: affine restrictions plus strong terminals | Prove the positive-surplus coverage theorem following (1), with any fixed `delta>0` | Test literal identifications and short affine substitutions on low-fanout parity interfaces with nonlinear continuations; reject a rule if new parity construction, rank loss, or a terminal cost consumes its claimed surplus |
| Conditional: one-way or formula terminals | Certify interfaces with `R_B<=2^b`, or a formula conversion with explicit duplication cost, on a class forced by failed restriction rules | INDEX must remain outside the one-way claim; parity must respect both its basis and sharing costs; stop if gate charges have uncontrolled multiplicity |
| Diagnostic only at present | Raw sensitivity, formula shrinkage, or ordinary block composition as universal `B2` bounds | Equations (2), (3), and the product-fiber rectangle already falsify the direct transfers |

The [finite checker](data/check_claims.py) verifies the sixteen-gate classification,
parity restriction expectations from exhaustive truth tables, sharp INDEX cofactor
counts, product-fiber rectangles, and the surplus benchmark. It checks examples
and arithmetic; the displayed general deductions have the proofs above. None of
these establishes universal coverage, an improved unrestricted coefficient, or a
new average-case terminal theorem. Historical literature claims are attributed;
the accounting synthesis and counterexamples carry no claim of novelty.
